## 1. National Assessment & Subject Selection Rules (Post-PWPER Guidelines)
* **Total Examinable Subjects:** Exactly **7 subjects** (4 Compulsory Core + 3 Pathway Electives).
* **Mathematics Split Rule:**
  * **Core Mathematics:** Compulsory for all learners taking the **STEM Pathway**. Focuses on advanced algebra, calculus, coordinate geometry, trigonometry, and statistical probability.
  * **Essential Mathematics:** Tailored for learners taking the **Social Sciences** and **Arts & Sports Science** pathways. Focuses on consumer numeracy, business accounting, tax computation, financial literacy, and data modeling.
* **National Certification:** Awarded via the **Kenya Senior School Education Assessment (KSSEA)** administered by KNEC:
  * **15%** from Grade 10 School-Based Assessment (SBA).
  * **15%** from Grade 11 School-Based Assessment (SBA).
  * **70%** from Grade 12 Summative KSSEA Examination.
  * Successful completion leads to the **Kenya Certificate of Basic Education (KCBE)**.

## 2. In-Depth Breakdown of Senior School CBC Optional / Elective Subjects

Learners select **three (3) optional subjects** aligned with their intended university degree clusters or technical careers.

### 2.1 PATHWAY A: SCIENCE, TECHNOLOGY, ENGINEERING & MATHEMATICS (STEM)

#### Track 1: Pure Sciences
1. **Physics:**
   * *Core Strands:* Mechanics & Motion (Kinematics, Dynamics, Statics), Thermal Physics & Thermodynamics, Waves & Optics, Electricity & Magnetism, Atomic & Modern Physics (Photoelectric effect, X-rays, Nuclear energy), Space and Geophysics (Renewable energy systems, Olkaria geothermal dynamics).
   * *Target Careers:* Civil, Mechanical, Electrical & Aerospace Engineering; Architecture; Astrophysics; Physical Sciences.
2. **Chemistry:**
   * *Core Strands:* Atomic Structure & Chemical Bonding, Quantitative Chemistry (The Mole concept, Stoichiometry, Volumetric Analysis), Chemical Kinetics & Energetics, Periodic Trends, Acids, Bases & Salts, Organic Chemistry (Hydrocarbons, Polymers, Biomolecules), Industrial Chemistry & Metallurgy (Extraction of metals, chemical environmental safety).
   * *Target Careers:* Medicine, Surgery, Pharmacy, Chemical Engineering, Biotechnology, Agricultural Sciences.
3. **Biology:**
   * *Core Strands:* Cell Biology & Cytology, Physiology of Plants and Animals (Nutrition, Respiration, Transport, Excretion, Coordination/Nervous control), Genetics, Heredity & Molecular Biology, Ecology & Environmental Conservation (Kenyan wildlife ecosystems, Lake Victoria water ecology), Biotechnology and Genetic Engineering.
   * *Target Careers:* Human Medicine, Nursing, Veterinary Medicine, Genetics, Agronomy, Marine Biology.
4. **Advanced / Pure Mathematics (Elective Option for Specialist STEM):**
   * *Core Strands:* Differential & Integral Calculus, Matrices and Linear Transformations, Vectors in 3D, Complex Numbers, Further Trigonometry, Discrete Mathematics.
   * *Target Careers:* Actuarial Science, Theoretical Physics, Data Science, Pure Mathematics, Computer Engineering.

#### Track 2: Applied Sciences & Computing
5. **Computer Studies / Computer Science:**
   * *Core Strands:* Computer Architecture & Hardware Systems, Data Representation (Binary, Hexadecimal, ASCII), Networking & Cybersecurity, Algorithm Design, Logic & Flowcharts, Software Development & Programming (Python, Web Technologies), Database Management Systems (SQL), Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies.
   * *Target Careers:* Software Engineering, Cyber Defense, Artificial Intelligence, Cloud Architecture, Systems Analysis.
6. **Agriculture:**
   * *Core Strands:* Crop Production and Modern Agronomy (Horticulture, Agroforestry, Hydroponics), Animal Production and Veterinary Hygiene (Livestock nutrition, breeding, dairy tech), Soil Science and Irrigation Systems, Agricultural Economics, Marketing and Farm Management, Agri-Technology and Mechanization.
   * *Target Careers:* Agribusiness, Food Security Engineering, Veterinary Science, Agricultural Economics.
7. **Home Science:**
   * *Core Strands:* Nutrition and Dietetics, Food Preparation and Culinary Arts, Maternal and Child Health Care, Textile Science and Clothing Technology, Consumer Education and Household Economics.
   * *Target Careers:* Dietetics & Clinical Nutrition, Food Technology, Hospitality Management, Fashion Design.

#### Track 3: Technical & Engineering Studies
8. **Drawing and Design:**
   * *Core Strands:* Plane and Solid Geometry, Orthographic and Isometric Projections, Engineering Drawing Standards, Architectural Drafting, Computer-Aided Design (CAD).
   * *Target Careers:* Mechanical Drafting, Architecture, Structural Engineering, Industrial Design.
9. **Aviation Technology:**
   * *Core Strands:* Principles of Flight (Aerodynamics, Lift, Drag, Thrust), Aircraft Structures and Powerplants, Navigation and Flight Instruments, Air Traffic Control and Meteorology, Aviation Safety and Regulations.
   * *Target Careers:* Commercial Piloting, Aerospace Engineering, Air Traffic Management, Aircraft Maintenance.
10. **Building and Construction:**
    * *Core Strands:* Building Materials (Concrete, Timber, Masonry), Site Preparation and Surveying, Foundations and Wall Construction, Roofing Systems, Plumbing and Drainage, Environmental Safety and Quantity Surveying.
    * *Target Careers:* Construction Management, Civil Engineering, Quantity Surveying.
11. **Electrical and Electronics Technology:**
    * *Core Strands:* AC/DC Circuit Analysis, Electrical Installation and Wiring Regulations, Electronics Components (Semiconductors, Integrated Circuits, Sensors), Renewable Energy Systems (Solar PV, Wind installations), Electrical Maintenance.
    * *Target Careers:* Electrical Engineering, Telecommunications, Renewable Energy Technician.
12. **Metalwork / Power Mechanics / Woodwork / Marine Technology:**
    * Specialized vocational-technical electives focused on machine fabrication, automotive engineering, structural joinery, and maritime marine engines.

## 2. Cambridge Mathematics (0580 & 0607)

### 2.1 The Critical 2025–2027 Rebalanced Structure
Starting in 2025, CAIE overhauled Syllabus 0580:
* **The 50/50 Non-Calculator Split:**
  * **Paper 1 (Core)** and **Paper 2 (Extended)** are strictly **NON-CALCULATOR**.
  * **Paper 3 (Core)** and **Paper 4 (Extended)** permit scientific calculators.
* **New Mandatory Content in Non-Calculator Papers:**
  * **Surds:** Simplifying $\sqrt{ab}$, expanding expressions, and rationalising denominators ($\frac{a}{\sqrt{b}}$, $\frac{a}{b \pm \sqrt{c}}$).
  * **Exact Trigonometric Values:** Memorized surds/fractions for $0^\circ, 30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ$ for $\sin, \cos, \tan$.
  * **Formal Functions:** $f(x), fg(x), f^{-1}(x)$, domain and range.
  * Official formula sheet printed on page 2 of question papers.

### 2.2 Core vs. Extended Tier Boundaries
* **Core Tier (Papers 1 & 3):** Capped at **Grade C** (in 9–1: Grade 5).
* **Extended Tier (Papers 2 & 4):** Covers Core + Extended Supplement. Awards **$A^*$ to E** (in 9–1: Grades 9 to 3). Includes 3D trigonometry, circle theorems, vectors with geometric proofs, and histograms with unequal class widths ($\text{Frequency Density} = \frac{\text{Frequency}}{\text{Class Width}}$).

### 2.3 Cambridge Marking Taxonomy
* **M (Method Mark):** For valid mathematical process; immune to arithmetic slips.
* **A (Accuracy Mark):** For correct intermediate/final result; strictly dependent on preceding M mark ($M0 \implies A0$).
* **B (Independent Mark):** Independent of method (can be earned with no working shown).
* **SC (Special Case):** Partial credit for anticipated common errors.
* **FT (Follow Through):** Prevents double-penalization for earlier slips.
* **General Accuracy Rules:** Non-exact values rounded to **3 significant figures (3 s.f.)**; angles in degrees to **1 decimal place (1 d.p.)**. Premature rounding forfeits accuracy marks.

## 3. Cambridge Pure Sciences (Physics 0625, Chemistry 0620, Biology 0610)

### 3.1 Paper Structure
* **Paper 1 (Core MCQ)** or **Paper 2 (Extended MCQ):** 45 mins | 40 marks | 30% weighting.
* **Paper 3 (Core Theory)** or **Paper 4 (Extended Theory):** 1 hr 15 mins | 80 marks | 50% weighting.
* **Paper 5 (Practical Test)** or **Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical - ATP):** 1 hr | 40 marks | 20% weighting. *(Paper 6 is a written exam sat by >80% of Kenyan candidates testing experimental design, variables, and data plotting).*

### 3.2 Key Syllabus Highlights (2023–2026 Updates)
* **Physics 0625:** Gravitational field strength $g = 9.8\text{ N/kg}$; **Topic 6 Space Physics (Mandatory)**: Orbital speed $v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}$, planetary orbits, stellar life cycles (Protostar $\to$ Main Sequence $\to$ Red Giant/Supergiant $\to$ White Dwarf/Supernova), Redshift, CMBR, Hubble’s Law ($v = H_0 d$), and universe age estimation ($t \approx 1/H_0$).
* **Chemistry 0620:** Stoichiometry & the mole ($n = m/M_r$, gas volume $24\text{ dm}^3$ at r.t.p., solution concentration $C = n/V$), electrochemistry, fuel cells, Le Chatelier's equilibrium (Haber & Contact processes), redox with acidified $\text{KMnO}_4$ and aqueous $\text{KI}$.
* **Biology 0610:** 21 core topics with strict examiner vocabulary: enzymes are **denatured** (never "killed"); osmosis requires *"water potential gradient"* across a *"partially permeable membrane"*; active transport requires *"carrier proteins"* moving particles *"against a concentration gradient"*.

### 3.3 Paper 6 Alternative to Practical (ATP) Rules
* Table headings must use solidus notation: `Quantity / Unit` (e.g., `Time / s`, `Temperature / °C`).
* Best-fit line must be a single, smooth, thin line (straight or curved).
* 6-mark planning question requires: independent, dependent, and two controlled variables, specific apparatus, numbered steps, safety precautions, and data processing.

## 4. Cambridge English (0500) & Computer Science (0478)

### 4.1 First Language English (0500)
* **Paper 1 (Reading - 50%):**
  * *120-Word Summary Rule (Q1f):* 10 content points + 5 writing marks. Exceeding 140 words caps the writing band; verbatim lifting scores zero.
  * *Writer’s Effects (Q2d):* Deconstruct connotations and reader impact without generic "feature-spotting".
  * *Extended Response (Q3):* Bullet 3 requires speculative evaluation and extrapolation.
* **Paper 2 (Directed Writing & Composition - 50%):**
  * *Directed Writing:* Evaluate and synthesize arguments from two stimulus texts (never just summarize).
  * *Composition:* Strict separation between Descriptive (static, camera-pan, sensory focus) and Narrative (Freytag's dynamic dramatic arc).

### 4.2 Computer Science (0478)
* **Section 1: Computer Systems:**
  * 6-step Von Neumann Fetch-Decode-Execute (FDE) cycle (PC, MAR, MDR, CIR, ACC registers; Address, Data, Control buses).
  * Automated control loop: $\text{Sensor} \to \text{ADC} \to \text{Microprocessor comparison} \to \text{Actuator}$.
* **Section 2: Algorithms, Logic & Programming:**
  * Cambridge pseudocode standards, change-only trace tables, two's complement sign-bit rules.
  * **Compulsory 15-Mark Unseen Scenario Question (Paper 2):** 5-phase blueprint (Declare/Initialize $\to$ Master loop $\to$ Input validation loop $\to$ Algorithmic processing $\to$ User output).

## 5. Official CAIE Command Words & Socratic Rules

| Command Word | CAIE Definition | Elingu Socratic Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **State** | Express in clear terms. | Prompt for a direct, single-phrase factual recall (1 mark). |
| **Describe** | State points / give characteristics and main features. | Prompt for *what* happens in sequential order without requiring causal mechanism. |
| **Explain** | Set out purposes or reasons; state *why* and/or *how*. | **Never accept descriptive answers.** Insist on the underlying scientific or mathematical mechanism. |
| **Calculate** | Work out from given facts, figures, or information. | Guide student through formula substitution, intermediate working, and 3 s.f. rounding. |
| **Determine** | Establish with certainty. | Demand mathematical deduction or formula proof; reject visual guessing. |
| **Suggest** | Apply knowledge to a novel situation. | Guide student to bridge familiar principles to an unfamiliar scenario. |
| **Deduce** | Draw conclusions from data or graphs. | Ask what trend, relationship, or scientific law the data proves. |
| **Show (that)** | Provide structured evidence leading to a given result. | The final answer is given! Guide the student through every intermediate deduction step. |
