## 1. Executive Summary & Strategic Vision for Elingu

Elingu is designed to be East Africa's premier Socratic AI learning companion. While Kenya's Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC) represents Elingu's mass-market public and private school foundation, the **British Curriculum (Cambridge CAIE and Pearson Edexcel)** represents our **highest-margin, highest-purchasing-power tier**.

### Why IGCSE is Critical for Elingu:
1. **Unprecedented Willingness to Pay:** Parents with children in British international schools in Nairobi, Mombasa, Nakuru, and Eldoret pay between **KES 450,000 and KES 3,200,000+** annually per child in tuition. Supplementary home tutoring is already normalized, costing parents **KES 30,000 to KES 60,000 per month**. An AI Socratic subscription at **KES 3,500/month** or **KES 8,900/term** is perceived as an affordable, high-value insurance policy on their multi-million shilling schooling investment.
2. **Acute Academic Rigidity:** Cambridge examinations are marked against uncompromising, keyword-precise mark schemes. Students frequently understand concepts intuitively but lose 30–50% of available marks due to missing syllabus-mandated vocabulary or command-word misinterpretation.
3. **The Socratic Differentiation:** Generic AI tools (like raw ChatGPT or Photomath) immediately regurgitate the final answer, creating an "illusion of competence" that breaks down in exam halls. Elingu's strict Socratic dialogue—forcing students to derive equations, explain causal mechanisms, and identify keywords themselves—delivers durable, genuine exam readiness.

### 2.4 The Kenyan "Grade 9 Flight" Transition Dynamic
A critical demographic pattern in Kenya is the **Grade 9 Flight**:
1. **The Structural Trigger:** Due to teething problems, laboratory shortages, and teacher allocations in public/mid-tier Kenyan Junior Secondary Schools (JSS), many families view **Grade 9 (age 14) as an exit point**.
2. **The Transition:** After taking the national KJSEA assessment at the end of Grade 9, students transfer into **Year 10 (IGCSE)** in British curriculum schools rather than progressing to CBC Grade 10 Senior School.
3. **The Cognitive Shock:** These students encounter an immediate **"Cognitive Rigor Deficit"**:
   * *Mathematics:* Transitioning from basic procedural calculation to multi-step algebraic proofs, surds, and geometric circle theorems.
   * *Sciences:* Lack of experience with stoichiometric mole ratios ($n = m/M_r$), chemical reaction mechanisms, and the rigorous experimental conventions of **Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical)**.
   * *English:* Struggling with Cambridge 0500's strict 120-word summary limit and analytical evaluation of contrasting texts.
4. **Opportunity for Elingu:** This transition cohort represents our highest-converting customer segment for an on-demand Socratic tutor that bridges the gap between domestic foundational skills and international exam expectations.

## 5. Lower Secondary Framework (Years 7, 8, 9 / Stages 7–9 / Checkpoint)

### 5.1 Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics (Syllabus 0862)
The framework is structured into four core strands unified by **Thinking and Working Mathematically (TWM)**:
1. **Number:**
   * *Stage 7:* Prime factor decomposition, HCF/LCM, operations with negative integers, index notation, rounding to decimal places and significant figures, fractions/decimals/percentages equivalences, dividing in a ratio.
   * *Stage 8:* Laws of indices ($a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}, a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}, (a^m)^n = a^{mn}$), standard form for large numbers ($A \times 10^n$), percentage multipliers, reverse percentages in simple contexts, rates of change (speed, density).
   * *Stage 9:* Negative and zero indices ($a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}, a^0 = 1$), fractional indices ($a^{1/2} = \sqrt{a}, a^{1/3} = \sqrt[3]{a}$), standard form for small numbers ($A \times 10^{-n}$), rational vs. irrational numbers, compound interest, upper and lower bounds.
2. **Algebra:**
   * *Stage 7:* Collecting like terms, expanding single brackets $a(bx + c)$, linear equations in one unknown, sequences (term-to-term and position-to-term rules), plotting $y = mx + c$.
   * *Stage 8:* Expanding $-a(bx - c)$ and $(x + a)(x + b)$, factorising with common monomial factor, rearranging linear formulae, equations with unknowns on both sides, $n$th term of linear sequences ($u_n = dn + c$).
   * *Stage 9:* Expanding $(ax + b)(cx + d)$, factorising quadratics ($x^2 + bx + c$ and difference of two squares $x^2 - a^2$), simplifying algebraic fractions, simultaneous linear equations (elimination and substitution), solving linear inequalities, plotting quadratic curves ($y = ax^2 + bx + c$) and reciprocal graphs ($y = k/x$).
3. **Geometry and Measure:**
   * *Stage 7:* Angle properties of intersecting and parallel lines (alternate, corresponding, co-interior), perimeter and area of composite 2D shapes, volume and surface area of cuboids, reflections and rotations in coordinate planes.
   * *Stage 8:* Interior/exterior angles of polygons (Sum of interior $= (n-2) \times 180^\circ$; exterior $= 360^\circ$), circle geometry ($C = 2\pi r, A = \pi r^2$), surface area and volume of prisms and cylinders, Pythagoras' Theorem in 2D ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$), 3-figure bearings.
   * *Stage 9:* Congruence and similarity, Pythagoras in 3D boxes, basic trigonometry ($\sin, \cos, \tan$ in right-angled triangles), enlargement with fractional and negative scale factors, midpoint and length of line segments.
4. **Statistics and Probability:**
   * *Stage 7:* Statistical enquiry cycle, frequency tables, bar charts, pictograms, pie charts, mean/median/mode/range, probability scale (0 to 1).
   * *Stage 8:* Grouped frequency, estimating mean of grouped data using midpoints, scatter graphs, correlation and lines of best fit, sample space diagrams, Venn diagrams, tree diagrams for independent events.
   * *Stage 9:* Cumulative frequency curves, estimating median and Interquartile Range (IQR), box plots (box-and-whisker), tree diagrams for dependent events (without replacement), conditional probability from two-way tables.
5. **Thinking & Working Mathematically (TWM) Pillars:**
   * *Specialising & Generalising* (testing examples $\leftrightarrow$ deriving overarching algebraic rules).
   * *Conjecturing & Convincing* (hypothesising $\leftrightarrow$ constructing formal proofs).
   * *Characterising & Classifying* (identifying properties $\leftrightarrow$ categorising mathematical objects).
   * *Critiquing & Improving* (evaluating methods $\leftrightarrow$ optimising solutions).

### 5.2 Cambridge Lower Secondary Science (Syllabus 0893)
Structured across four content strands, plus **Thinking and Working Scientifically (TWS)** and **Science in Context**:
* **Biology:** Cell ultrastructure (plant vs. animal cells, specialized cells); organ systems (digestive, respiratory, circulatory, nervous, endocrine, human reproductive system); photosynthesis and mineral nutrition; feeding relationships, bioaccumulation, ecosystems; variation and natural selection fundamentals.
* **Chemistry:** Particle model of solids, liquids, gases; changes of state and heating curves; pure substances vs. mixtures; solubility and separation techniques (chromatography, distillation); atomic structure, isotopes, and the Periodic Table; word and balanced symbol equations; exothermic and endothermic reactions; acids, bases, indicators, and salt preparation; rates of reaction (temperature, concentration, surface area, catalysts).
* **Physics:** Contact and non-contact forces; mass vs. weight ($W = mg$); Hooke's Law; speed and distance-time graphs; turning forces and moments ($M = F \times d$); pressure in fluids and surfaces ($p = F/A$); energy stores and conservation of energy; heat transfer (conduction, convection, radiation); light (reflection, refraction, lenses) and sound (waves, pitch, loudness); current, voltage, resistance, and circuits; electromagnets.
* **Earth and Space:** Structure of the Earth (crust, mantle, core); rock cycle (igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic); atmosphere, water cycle, and carbon cycle; Solar System, orbital periods, eclipses, Moon phases; stellar evolution and galaxies.
* **Thinking & Working Scientifically (TWS):**
  - Models and representations.
  - Scientific enquiry (purpose, hypothesis, variable control: independent, dependent, controlled).
  - Practical data collection and safety.
  - Analysis, evaluation, anomalous data detection, and evidence-based conclusions.

## 6. Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics (0580 & 0607)

### 6.1 The Critical 2025–2027 Rebalanced Structure (50/50 Non-Calculator Split)
Starting with the **2025 examination series**, CAIE instituted the most significant overhaul of IGCSE Mathematics 0580 in over a decade:
1. **Introduction of a Non-Calculator Paper:** Paper 1 (Core) and Paper 2 (Extended) are strictly **NON-CALCULATOR**.
2. **Equal 50% / 50% Weighting:** Equal marks and duration between non-calculator and calculator papers.
3. **Official Formula Sheet:** Printed on page 2 of every examination paper.

```
0580 Examination Tiers (2025 - 2027)
├── CORE TIER (Grades C to G / Max Achievable Grade: C)
│   ├── Paper 1: Non-Calculator (1 hr 30 mins | 80 Marks | 50% Weighting)
│   └── Paper 3: Calculator Allowed (1 hr 30 mins | 80 Marks | 50% Weighting)
└── EXTENDED TIER (Grades A* to E / Max Achievable Grade: A*)
    ├── Paper 2: Non-Calculator (2 hrs 00 mins | 100 Marks | 50% Weighting)
    └── Paper 4: Calculator Allowed (2 hrs 00 mins | 100 Marks | 50% Weighting)
```

#### New Mandatory Topics in the 2025 Syllabus:
* **Surds:** Simplifying $\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}$, expanding brackets containing surds, and rationalising denominators ($\frac{a}{\sqrt{b}}$ and $\frac{a}{b \pm \sqrt{c}}$) tested on Paper 2 without a calculator.
* **Exact Trigonometric Values:** Candidates must know exact fractional/surd values for $0^\circ, 30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ$ for $\sin, \cos, \tan$.
* **Formal Function Notation:** Explicit testing of $f(x)$, composite functions $fg(x) = f(g(x))$, inverse functions $f^{-1}(x)$, domain, and range.
* **Removed Topics:** Linear programming, box-and-whisker plots, and congruence proofs have been trimmed or removed from 0580.

### 6.2 Core vs. Extended Curriculum & Tier Boundaries

| Content Strand | Core Curriculum (Capped at Grade C) | Extended Supplement (Grades A* to E) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Number** | Basic arithmetic, fractions, decimals, prime factors, standard form, simple interest, percentages, ratio. | **Surds**, fractional indices ($a^{p/q} = \sqrt[q]{a^p}$), **bounds in calculations** ($\text{UB} = \frac{\text{UB}_a}{\text{LB}_b}$), compound interest formula $A = P(1 + r/100)^n$, exponential growth/decay, reverse percentages. |
| **Algebra & Graphs** | Linear expressions, single brackets, linear equations, simultaneous equations, plotting linear/quadratic graphs. | Factorising $ax^2 + bx + c$, completing the square $a(x+p)^2 + q$, quadratic formula, algebraic fractions, rearranging complex formulae, formal **functions ($fg(x), f^{-1}(x)$)**, direct and inverse proportion ($y \propto x^n, y \propto \frac{1}{x^n}$), tangents for gradient, area under speed-time graphs. |
| **Coordinate Geometry** | Midpoint, gradient $m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$, line equation $y = mx + c$. | Exact length of line segment, parallel lines ($m_1 = m_2$), **perpendicular lines ($m_1 \times m_2 = -1$)**, equations of perpendicular bisectors. |
| **Geometry** | Parallel line angles, polygon angles, circle vocabulary, basic similarity. | **Circle Theorems** (7 theorems including alternate segment theorem), formal geometric proofs, area scale factor ($k^2$) and volume scale factor ($k^3$). |

### 6.3 Cambridge Marking Taxonomy: M, A, B, SC, and FT Marks
Every mark in a Cambridge Mathematics mark scheme belongs to a strict category:
* **M (Method Mark):** Awarded for showing a correct, valid mathematical step or formula substitution. **M marks are independent of arithmetic slips.** If a student writes the correct quadratic formula substitution but makes a mental addition error, the M1 mark is secured.
* **A (Accuracy Mark):** Awarded for reaching a correct intermediate or final answer. **A marks are strictly dependent on preceding M marks.** Scoring `M0` automatically results in `A0` (`M0 \implies A0`). An accuracy mark cannot be awarded for a lucky correct number derived from flawed working.
* **B (Independent Mark):** Awarded for an entirely correct statement, coordinate, or numerical result **independent of method**. B marks can be earned even if no working is shown.
* **SC (Special Case):** Awarded for specific anticipated student errors or alternate non-standard paths, allowing partial credit.
* **FT (Follow Through):** Awarded when a student uses their own incorrect earlier value correctly in subsequent steps. Prevents double-penalization for a single slip.
* **cao (Correct Answer Only):** No Follow Through permitted; only the exact value scores.
* **oe (Or Equivalent):** Equivalent fractions, decimals, or algebraic forms accepted.
* **nfww (Not From Wrong Working):** The answer must be achieved through valid logic.
* **General Accuracy Rules:** Non-exact values must be rounded to **3 significant figures (3 s.f.)**; angles in degrees to **1 decimal place (1 d.p.)**. Premature rounding during intermediate steps that distorts the final answer forfeits the accuracy mark.

### 6.4 Top 10 Student Misconceptions & Pitfalls from Official CAIE Examiner Reports
1. **The Single Transformation Violation:** In questions stating *"Describe fully the single transformation..."*, writing two transformations (e.g., *"Reflection in $y = 0$ followed by translation $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$"*) scores **ZERO marks for the entire question**, even if one part is correct.
2. **Negative Number Exponent Precedence:** Confusing $-3^2 = -9$ with $(-3)^2 = 9$. When substituting $x = -5$ into $b^2 - 4ac$, writing $-25 - 4ac$ destroys the calculation.
3. **Sign Errors in Distributing Negatives:** In algebraic fractions, writing $\frac{5(x-1) - 3(x+2)}{(x+2)(x-1)} = \frac{5x - 5 - 3x + 6}{\dots}$ instead of $-3x - 6$.
4. **Histograms: Frequency vs. Frequency Density:** Plotting raw frequency on the vertical axis for unequal class widths instead of calculating $\text{Frequency Density} = \frac{\text{Frequency}}{\text{Class Width}}$. (In histograms, Frequency = Area of the bar).
5. **Compound Upper and Lower Bounds:** To maximize a fraction $\frac{A}{B}$, candidates calculate $\frac{\text{UB}_A}{\text{UB}_B}$ instead of $\frac{\text{UB}_A}{\text{LB}_B}$.
6. **3D Trigonometry Projections:** Choosing the wrong edge of a pyramid base rather than the half-diagonal when projecting the slant edge onto the horizontal base.
7. **Premature Rounding Penalties:** Rounding intermediate sides to 2 s.f. midway through a trigonometry problem, leading to an inaccurate final angle.
8. **Reverse Percentages Misconception:** Adding 20% to a discounted price of \$160 rather than setting up $0.80x = 160 \implies x = \$200$.
9. **Linear vs. Area vs. Volume Scale Factors ($k, k^2, k^3$):** Assuming area doubles when length doubles, rather than quadrupling ($k^2 = 4$).
10. **Probability Without Replacement:** Forgetting to reduce both numerator and denominator on the second branch of a probability tree (e.g., writing $\frac{4}{10} \times \frac{3}{10}$ instead of $\frac{4}{10} \times \frac{3}{9}$).

### 7.2 Physics (0625): Core Topics & Space Physics
* **Updated Gravitational Constant:** $g = 9.8\text{ N/kg}$ (or $9.8\text{ m/s}^2$).
* **Topic 1: Motion, Forces & Energy:** Speed, velocity, acceleration; speed-time graphs (gradient = acceleration, area = distance); Hooke's Law ($F = k\Delta x$); Newton's laws ($F = ma$); centripetal forces; momentum ($p = mv$) and impulse ($J = F\Delta t = \Delta(mv)$); mechanical work ($W = Fd$), power ($P = W/t$), and efficiency; fluid pressure ($\Delta p = \rho g \Delta h$).
* **Topic 2: Thermal Physics:** Kinetic particle model; Brownian motion; thermal expansion; specific heat capacity ($c = \frac{\Delta E}{m\Delta T}$); latent heat ($L_f, L_v$); conduction, convection, radiation (matte black = best emitter/absorber).
* **Topic 3: Waves:** Wave speed ($v = f\lambda$); reflection, refraction (Snell's Law: $n = \frac{\sin i}{\sin r} = \frac{c}{v}$); total internal reflection ($\sin c = \frac{1}{n}$); thin converging lenses; electromagnetic spectrum; sound waves and echoes ($v = \frac{2d}{t}$).
* **Topic 4: Electricity & Magnetism:** Electric charge ($Q = It$); Ohm's Law ($V = IR$); series and parallel circuit rules; electrical power ($P = IV = I^2 R$); potential dividers ($V_{out} = V_{in} \frac{R_1}{R_1+R_2}$); transducers (LDRs, thermistors); electromagnetic induction, AC generator, DC motor; transformers ($\frac{V_p}{V_s} = \frac{N_p}{N_s}$ and $I_p V_p = I_s V_s$).
* **Topic 5: Nuclear Physics:** Rutherford scattering (dense, positive nucleus); alpha, beta, gamma properties; radioactive decay equations; half-life ($t_{1/2}$) calculations; nuclear fission (splitting heavy nuclei) vs. nuclear fusion (joining hydrogen nuclei in stars).
* **Topic 6: Space Physics (Mandatory Topic):**
  * Orbital speed: $v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}$.
  * Solar System structure, planetary orbits, elliptical comet orbits.
  * Stellar life cycle: Nebula $\to$ Protostar $\to$ Main sequence $\to$ Red Giant (Sun-like) $\to$ Planetary Nebula $\to$ White Dwarf; or Red Supergiant $\to$ Supernova $\to$ Neutron Star / Black Hole.
  * Cosmic expansion: Redshift of distant galaxies; Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation (CMBR); Hubble’s Law ($v = H_0 d$ with $H_0 \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-18}\text{ s}^{-1}$); Age of the Universe ($t \approx \frac{1}{H_0} \approx 13.8$ billion years).

### 7.3 Chemistry (0620): Stoichiometry, The Mole Concept & Chemical Processes
* **States of Matter & Separation:** Diffusion rates dependent on molecular mass ($M_r$); chromatography ($R_f = \frac{\text{distance by substance}}{\text{distance by solvent front}}$); fractional distillation.
* **Atomic Structure & Bonding:** Ionic bonding (electrostatic attraction between cations and anions); covalent bonding (sharing electrons); giant covalent structures (diamond: 4 bonds, hard; graphite: 3 bonds, layers, delocalized electrons, conducts); metallic bonding (cations in sea of delocalized electrons).
* **Stoichiometry & Mole Calculations:**
  * Mass equation: $n = \frac{m}{M_r}$.
  * Gas volume at r.t.p.: $V = n \times 24\text{ dm}^3$ ($24\,000\text{ cm}^3$).
  * Solution concentration: $C = \frac{n}{V} = \frac{m}{M_r \times V}$ (where $V$ is in $\text{dm}^3$).
  * Empirical vs. molecular formulas; limiting reactants; percentage yield ($\frac{\text{actual}}{\text{theoretical}} \times 100\%$) and purity.
* **Electrochemistry:** Anode (oxidation) vs. Cathode (reduction); electrolysis of molten $\text{PbBr}_2$, concentrated aqueous $\text{NaCl}$ (brine: $\text{H}_2$ at cathode, $\text{Cl}_2$ at anode, $\text{NaOH}$ remains); copper refining; hydrogen-oxygen fuel cells ($2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
* **Chemical Energetics & Kinetics:** $\Delta H = \sum (\text{Bonds Broken}) - \sum (\text{Bonds Formed})$; collision theory (activation energy $E_a$, collision frequency, kinetic energy); catalysts.
* **Equilibria:** Dynamic equilibrium; Le Chatelier’s Principle; industrial processes:
  * *Haber Process:* $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$ ($450^\circ\text{C}, 200\text{ atm}, \text{Fe}$ catalyst).
  * *Contact Process:* $2\text{SO}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{SO}_3$ ($450^\circ\text{C}, 1\text{–}2\text{ atm}, \text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ catalyst).
* **Acids, Bases, and Salts:** Proton donor/acceptor definitions; preparation of soluble salts (excess insoluble base/carbonate or titration) and insoluble salts (precipitation).
* **Organic Chemistry:** Fractional distillation of petroleum; alkanes (substitution with $\text{Cl}_2$ under UV); alkenes (catalytic cracking, addition with bromine water, steam, hydrogen); alcohols (fermentation vs. hydration of ethene); carboxylic acids and esterification; polymers (addition: poly(ethene); condensation: nylon [polyamide] and terylene [polyester]).

### 7.5 Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical - ATP) Golden Rules
Paper 6 accounts for **20% of the overall science grade**. Examiners enforce strict rules:
1. **Table Headings:** Must include both quantity and unit separated by a solidus (e.g., `"Time / s"`, `"Temperature / °C"`, `"Volume / cm³"`). Writing `"Time (s)"` or `"Time in sec"` is penalized in modern mark schemes.
2. **Graph Drawing Conventions:**
   * Independent variable on $x$-axis, dependent variable on $y$-axis.
   * Scales must be linear and occupy **more than 50% of the grid**.
   * Points plotted as a small, sharp cross ($\times$) or ringed dot ($\odot$) within $\pm \frac{1}{2}$ small square.
   * Line of best fit must be a single, smooth, thin line (straight or curved); thick, sketchy, or point-to-point lines forfeit marks.
3. **Significant Figures:** Calculated table values must match the decimal precision of the raw data.
4. **Experimental Design (The 6-Mark Planning Question):**
   * Identify independent, dependent, and at least two controlled variables.
   * Specify measuring apparatus with units (e.g., *"gas syringe measuring volume every 30 seconds"*).
   * Describe method in numbered steps.
   * State safety precautions (e.g., *"wear goggles due to corrosive acid"*).
   * Explain how data will be processed (e.g., *"repeat 3 times and calculate mean"*, *"plot graph of rate vs. temperature"*).

### 8.2 The 120-Word Summary Discipline (Paper 1 Question 1f)
* **Mark Allocation:** 10 Reading content points + 5 Writing marks.
* **Strict Length Rule:** Summaries must be **under 120 words** (acceptable range: 100–115 words).
* **Penalties:**
  * Writing $> 140$ words automatically caps the Writing score at Band 2 (max 3/5).
  * Writing $> 180$ words caps Writing at Band 1 (max 1/5).
* **Verbatim Lifting Penalty:** Copying whole clauses from the passage scores zero for Writing and invalidates Reading marks.
* **Format:** Continuous prose only (no bullet points, no introductory fluff, no moralizing conclusions).

### 8.3 Directed Writing & Composition: Synthesis vs. Narrative/Descriptive Mechanics
* **Section A Synthesis Principle:** Candidates must **never merely summarize Text A then summarize Text B**. High marks (Level 5/6) require weaving points together, exposing flawed assumptions, evaluating practical feasibility, and reaching a balanced conclusion.
* **Descriptive Composition (Frozen Time / Camera Pan):**
  * Focuses on sensory imagery (sight, sound, smell, texture), atmosphere, and microscopic zoom.
  * Structure: Establishing shot $\to$ Mid-ground $\to$ Micro zoom $\to$ Atmospheric shift $\to$ Cyclical reflection.
  * *Fatal Error:* The "Narrative Slip" (introducing gunshots, car chases, or action plots when descriptive ideas run dry).
* **Narrative Composition (Dynamic Arc):**
  * Follows Freytag's dramatic pyramid: Exposition $\to$ Inciting Incident $\to$ Rising Tension $\to$ Climax $\to$ Falling Action $\to$ Resolution.
  * Focuses on character motivation, realistic dialogue (properly punctuated with commas inside speech marks), and psychological conflict. Avoid cliché endings (*"and then I woke up and it was all a dream"*).

## 9. Cambridge IGCSE Computer Science (0478)

### 9.1 Section 1: Computer Systems & The 6-Step Automated Control Loop
* **Data Representation:** Denary, binary, hexadecimal; binary addition and overflow (carry out of MSB); two's complement for signed integers ($-128$ to $+127$); logical binary shifts; lossy vs. lossless compression; image file size ($\text{Width} \times \text{Height} \times \text{Colour Depth}$) and audio file size ($\text{Sample Rate} \times \text{Bit Depth} \times \text{Time}$).
* **Hardware & Von Neumann Architecture:**
  * Dedicated registers: **PC** (Program Counter), **MAR** (Memory Address Register), **MDR** (Memory Data Register), **CIR** (Current Instruction Register), **ACC** (Accumulator).
  * System buses: Address bus (unidirectional), Data bus (bidirectional), Control bus (bidirectional).
  * Fetch-Decode-Execute (FDE) cycle step-by-step.
* **The 6-Step Automated Control System Protocol (Strict Mark-Scheme Requirement):**
  1. Sensor measures continuous physical analogue data.
  2. Analogue-to-Digital Converter (ADC) converts measurement to digital signal.
  3. Digital data is transmitted to the **microprocessor**.
  4. The **microprocessor** compares the value against pre-stored threshold set-points.
  5. If the value exceeds or falls below threshold, the **microprocessor** sends a digital control signal.
  6. The **actuator** physically operates the mechanism (motor, valve, heater, buzzer).

### 9.2 Section 2: Algorithms, Logic & The 15-Mark Unseen Scenario Problem
* **Standard Algorithms:** Linear search, bubble sort, totalling, counting, finding maximum/minimum/average.
* **Trace Tables:** Strict Cambridge convention: **values must only be written on the row where they change**. Writing unchanged values across every line forfeits marks.
* **Boolean Logic:** NOT, AND, OR, NAND, NOR, XOR truth tables (up to 3 inputs / 8 rows).
* **The Compulsory 15-Mark Scenario Question (Paper 2):**
  * Candidates must write an algorithm in Cambridge Pseudocode or Python/Java/VB solving an unseen problem.
  * **Golden 5-Phase Architecture for 15/15 Marks:**
    1. *Phase 1:* Declare and initialize variables, arrays, and accumulators (`Total <- 0`, `Count <- 0`).
    2. *Phase 2:* Master loop construct (`FOR i <- 1 TO 30` or `WHILE ... DO`).
    3. *Phase 3:* Input prompt with post-condition validation loop (`REPEAT ... INPUT Val ... UNTIL Val >= 0`).
    4. *Phase 4:* Core algorithmic processing (updating running totals, comparing min/max, indexing 1D/2D arrays).
    5. *Phase 5:* Formatted user output outside loop (`OUTPUT "The average score is: ", Average`).
